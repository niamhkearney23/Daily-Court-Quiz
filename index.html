15 civil cases . 2 November 2026
Mohd Azizul bin Abd Wahab v Tan Sri Dato' Seri Abdul Hamid Bador (IG) & Ors
Question 1
Under Article 8(1) of the Federal Constitution, when will a law that treats one class of public officers differently from others survive an equality challenge?
(c) The Federal Court applied the reasonable classification test (Khong Teng Khen; Mior Rusli) and held police officers are a distinct class given their oath and constant duty under the Police Act 1967, so regulations 37 to 37D of the 1993 Regulations stood.
Vijayan a/l Subramaniam & Anor v Director General of National Registration Department & Ors
Question 2
Where a claimant makes out a prima facie case that a Malaysian-born child is stateless under section 1(e), Part II, Second Schedule, what happens to the burden of proof?
(a) The legal burden stays with the claimant, but once a prima facie case is shown the State must produce evidence of foreign citizenship, as a claimant need not prove a negative (Rosliza Ibrahim). The child was declared a citizen.
Malayan Banking Berhad v Exsa Sdn Bhd
Question 3
Under section 256(3) of the National Land Code, which of these is capable of being 'cause to the contrary' against an order for sale?
(d) Cause to the contrary is confined to the Low Lee Lian categories, which include a chargee's failure to satisfy a condition precedent. The other three points were all rejected and sale was ordered, with RM5,525,230.56 stated as due.
Nazrin Shahnaz binti Peer Mohamed (trading as Shahnaz Interiors) v Slob Sdn Bhd
Question 4
In a section 30 CIPAA direct payment claim, once the subcontractor proves non-payment, a written request and the principal's non-compliance, who must show whether money was due to the main contractor?
(b) The evidential burden on section 30(5) shifts to the principal under section 106 of the Evidence Act 1950. Slob produced no final account and was ordered to pay the RM740,794.41 adjudicated sum directly.
Phang Siew Cheng v Chong Weng Meng & Ors
Question 5
A Form 14A transfer is dated after the registered proprietor's death. What must his estate show to defeat the immediate transferee's title?
(c) A dead person cannot sign, so the instrument is void ab initio under section 340(2)(b) NLC and the immediate transferee holds only a defeasible title (Tan Ying Hong; Kamarulzaman). The land was restored to the estate.
Salhan bin Ahmad & Anor v Ketua Pengarah Jabatan Pendaftaran Negara, Malaysia & Ors
Question 6
For citizenship by operation of law, how does section 17, Part III, Second Schedule of the Federal Constitution treat an illegitimate child's 'father'?
(d) References to the father of an illegitimate child are read as references to the mother. With no proof of a lawful marriage and a Thai mother, the claim under section 1(b) failed (CTEB; Foo Toon Aik).
Lam Woon Paw v Lam Kuok Kinn
Question 7
Can a co-proprietor seek termination of a co-proprietorship under section 145(1)(b) of the National Land Code without first applying for partition?
(b) Following Ong Chin Hai, the limbs are alternatives. Partition of the 1,025 sq ft shophouse lot was barred by section 136(1)(f)(ii), and termination was ordered on reciprocal buy-out terms.
Badrul Hisham bin Ahmad v Naib Canselor Universiti Teknikal Malaysia Melaka (UTeM) & Ors
Question 8
At the leave stage of judicial review, the applicant's own papers show the challenge is out of time under Order 53 rule 3(6) and no extension was sought. The Attorney General raises no objection. What should the court do?
(a) Relying on Members of the Commission of Enquiry (FC) and Chin Mee Keong, the court applied the time bar itself. The only adverse decision dated from 16.07.2025 and time expired on 16.10.2025.
Tan Choong Han & Ors v Azmi bin Abdullah
Question 9
What must donors plead to sue for misuse of money they voluntarily donated?
(c) A voluntary donation is an absolute gift unless an express term limits it to a purpose. With no particulars pleaded, the claim for RM418,053 was struck out under Order 18 rule 19.
Tay Geak Choo @ Tai Chai Kee v Khor Swee Koung Holding Sdn Bhd & Ors
Question 10
Can a vendor that is still the registered proprietor support a private caveat over the land with its claim for the unpaid purchase price?
(d) Section 323(1) NLC protects those claiming title or a registrable interest, which a proprietor already holds (Mohamed Ali Abdul Razak). An unpaid price is personal (Wong Kuan Tan), so the caveat failed the first Luggage Distributors stage.
Yap Seng Peow & Ors v Wan Qi Yi
Question 11
How does the Limitation Act 1953 apply when a borrower sues to unwind disguised unlicensed moneylending?
(b) The court held a declaration of voidness has no limitation period, while restitution is a sum recoverable by written law under section 6(1)(d). RM13,166.35 was time-barred, leaving RM2,702,526 to be restored.
JCL Credit Leasing Sdn Bhd v Ooi Chung Yi
Question 12
A claimant sues for the lost opportunity to bring a claim against third parties. What must the lost claim have had?
(a) Applying Malayan Banking v Tan Soek Phee, a lost chance must have had a real and substantial prospect. Clause 7.1 gave no viable claim on the pleaded facts and the loss had not crystallised, so the RM15,027,927.14 claim was struck out.
Multazam Development Sdn Bhd v FGV Plantations (Malaysia) Sdn Bhd
Question 13
A tribunal decides a dispute that was referred to it, but on an argument a party says was never pleaded. Under the Arbitration Act 2005 this is:
(c) Excess of jurisdiction means deciding a new difference that was never referred. Here the extension of time ruling rested on Clause 49.1 alone, so the point was immaterial, and the award was enforced.
Syarikat Air Melaka Berhad v Fasihah binti Zamran & Ors (principal grounds)
Question 14
A pillion rider saw an unlit signboard just before the motorcycle hit it. Does that alone establish the rider's contributory negligence?
(b) Seeing a hazard is not the same as having a reasonable chance to avoid it, and the court cautioned against hindsight (Lim Kar Bee). The 10% finding was set aside and the utility held wholly liable.
Syarikat Air Melaka Berhad v Fasihah binti Zamran & Ors (further grounds)
Question 15
When assessing future care for a totally dependent plaintiff, what is the correct approach to a contingency deduction?
(c) Reading Wong Kuan Kay with Pantai Medical Centre, the court allowed only 10% for early receipt on the 343 future months and refused any deduction for public assistance, since eligibility is not receipt (section 28A(1)(c) Civil Law Act 1956).

What legal thread runs through today's decisions?
(b) From caveats and charges to citizenship, CIPAA, moneylending and personal injury damages, the courts asked whether the legal foundation for the right was actually there (a registered interest, pleaded particulars, a statutory condition) and looked past labels to the real transaction.
